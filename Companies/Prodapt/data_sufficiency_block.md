# Data Sufficiency — method + first timed set

start here tomorrow. Appears in the sample paper with four worked

## The format

You are NOT asked to answer the question. Only whether it CAN be
answered. Solving it fully is the trap that eats the clock.

    A   I alone is sufficient,  II alone is not
    B   II alone is sufficient, I alone is not
    C   EITHER alone is sufficient
    D   Both together are STILL not sufficient
    E   Both together are needed (neither alone works)

## The method — three steps, always in this order

1. Cover statement II. Can I answer using ONLY I?   Yes / No
2. Cover statement I.  Can I answer using ONLY II?  Yes / No
3. Only if both were No — do they work together?

    I alone   II alone   Answer
      yes       no         A
      no        yes        B
      yes       yes        C
      no        no         E if together works, D if not

## The two rules that decide most questions

1. When testing II, statement I DOES NOT EXIST. Carrying information
   from I into the test of II is the single biggest cause of misses.
   Physically cover it.

2. "Sufficient" means it pins down EXACTLY ONE answer. Two possible
   values = insufficient. Narrowing to "either 12 or 18" is worth
   nothing.

## Timed set — 10 questions, 60 s each. Target 10 minutes.

Answer with letters only. Do not solve fully.

1.  What is the value of x?
    I. x^2 = 49            II. x is positive

2.  How many students are in the class?
    I. If 4 more join, there'd be 30.
    II. The ratio of boys to girls is 3:2.

3.  Is n an even number?
    I. n is divisible by 6.    II. n is divisible by 3.

4.  What is Ravi's present age?
    I. Ravi is 5 years older than Sita.
    II. Sita will be 20 in 3 years.

5.  What is the area of the rectangle?
    I. The perimeter is 40 cm.  II. The length is twice the breadth.

6.  In a row of children, what is Priya's position from the left?
    I. There are 25 children in the row.
    II. Priya is 12th from the right.

7.  What is the two-digit number?
    I. The sum of its digits is 9.  II. The number is divisible by 9.

8.  Who is the tallest among P, Q, R?
    I. P is taller than Q.     II. R is shorter than Q.

9.  What is the speed of the train?
    I. It crosses a pole in 10 seconds.  II. It is 200 m long.

10. How much did the shopkeeper profit?
    I. He sold the item for Rs 1,200.
    II. His profit was 20% of cost price.
