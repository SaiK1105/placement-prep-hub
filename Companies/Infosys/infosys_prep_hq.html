Your play: Easy + Medium locked, then attack Hard.

  1. Read all 4 first — 10 minutes, no coding. You cannot choose well without seeing the full set, and the Easy is reported to have a lengthy statement that hides how simple it is.
  2. Bank Easy + Medium first. These are your floor — they're the difference between an offer and nothing. Do not open with the Hard because it's more interesting.
  3. Then spend everything left on the Hard. That's the ₹7 → ₹11–16 jump, and it's the realistic ceiling for one night's prep.
  4. Drop the Complex. Tree + range queries with nasty edge cases is not landing tomorrow, and a half-finished Complex scores the same as an unattempted one while costing you the Hard.

Time: 60 min average per question, but budget Easy 30 · Medium 45 · Hard 85 and keep 20 in reserve for testing.

Output contract — your known failure mode

"Passing sample test cases does not guarantee final acceptance."

Direct from the Jan 2026 candidate. Hidden tests and edge cases decide it — and this is exactly how you lost the TCS Easy this morning: correct logic, wrong output format. You printed a Python dict repr where a compact literal was required, and exact-match failed every case.

Tomorrow that mistake is worth ₹7 LPA. It is the single highest-probability way to lose a problem you have actually solved.

The rule — never print a container: 
print(d)              # {1: 1, 2: 1}   Python inserts spaces  ✗
print([50, 60])       # [50, 60]       same problem           ✗

print("{" + ",".join(f"{k}:{v}" for k,v in d.items()) + "}")   ✓
print("[" + ",".join(map(str, xs)) + "]")                      ✓
print(" ".join(map(str, xs)))                                  ✓
Pre-submit checklist — 60 seconds per question, every question:
  1. Re-read the required output format in the statement. Not the sample — the specification.
  2. Compare one line of your real output against it character by character. Spaces, brackets, separators, case.
  3. Run the edge cases nobody gives you: empty input, single element, zero, all-identical values, maximum constraint.
  4. Confirm you print nothing extra — no prompts, no labels, no debug lines left in.
  5. Check integer vs float. If the spec says 2 decimals, use f"{x:.2f}".

Unlike TCS, there's no reported no-edit-after-compile rule here, so you can iterate. But the same discipline still pays: with 3 hours and 3 problems, minutes spent debugging a formatting error are minutes stolen from the Hard.

Tonight's Infosys prep is also your Amazon prep.

Amazon's highest-frequency patterns — reported in over 90% of OAs — are arrays/strings, sliding window, two pointers, and hash-map frequency counting. Infosys's Medium slot is two-pointer + sliding window. These are the same exam twice. The 18th's Amazon block can therefore be short.

Two real 2026 SDE-Intern questions reported: "Warehouse Robots" and "Most Common Product Pair" (pair frequency analysis). Both are standard patterns wrapped in warehouse/inventory stories — Amazon dresses hashing and counting problems in logistics narratives. Read past the story to the structure.
Leadership Principles, the short version:
  • Bias for Action — when offered "gather more data" vs "act now and iterate", Amazon favours acting. Waiting or escalating reads as weak.
  • Ownership — never pick the option that hands the problem to another team.
  • Consistency — the survey repeats similar statements to catch contradictions. Answer as one coherent person.

Still deprioritising DP for Amazon. It's asked but far less often than the big four, and after tonight your DP time is better spent on the Infosys Hard shape than on breadth.
