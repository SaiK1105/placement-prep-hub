The only 7 moves you need

Every placement calculation is one of these seven. Learn the move, not the answer. Moves 1–5 build numbers up; moves 6–7 break them down and hold a chain together — that's where most people (and most marks) fall over.

1
The 10% anchor — to find any % of a number, first find 10% (just shift the decimal), then scale.
30% of 240 → 10% is 24 → ×3 → 72
5% of 380 → 10% is 38 → half → 19
35% of 400 → 10%=40 → ×3=120, +half of 40=20 → 140
1% works the same: shift twice. 7% of 300 → 1%=3 → ×7 → 21.
2
Percent = multiplier — you already know this one (you got 1.5 × 0.9 = 135 right!).
+20% → ×1.2 · −25% → ×0.75 · chain them for successive changes.
And for two changes there's the instant formula: a + b + ab/100 → +40, −20 → 40−20−8 = +12%.
3
Break it apart — big multiplications become small ones.
23 × 14 → 23×10 + 23×4 → 230 + 92 → 322
18 × 12 → 18×10 + 18×2 → 180+36 → 216
Always left to right: big chunk first, then the small one. Left-to-right is HOW mental math works — school taught you right-to-left because paper is patient. Your head isn't.
4
Round, then fix — compute with a friendly number, then repair the damage.
49 × 6 → 50×6 = 300, minus one 6 → 294
397 + 258 → 400+258 = 658, minus 3 → 655
75 × 12 → 75×12 is ¾ of 1200 → 900
5
Halve & double — trade a hard factor for an easy one.
16 × 35 → 8 × 70 → 560
25 × 48 → 100 × 12 → 1200 (25 = 100/4, so quarter the other number)
5 × anything → ×10, then halve. 5 × 86 → 860/2 → 430
6
Divide by un-multiplying — never long-divide in your head. Division is a question: "what times 36 gives 1584?" Three ways to answer it:

a) Split the divisor into factors. Divide twice by something easy instead of once by something ugly.
1584 ÷ 36 → ÷4 = 396 → ÷9 → 44
4032 ÷ 56 → ÷8 = 504 → ÷7 → 72
Same thing as cancelling a fraction before you compute it — 1584/36 → 396/9 → 44. Cancel first, always.

b) Scale both numbers. The answer doesn't change if you do the same thing to both.
168 ÷ 14 → halve both → 84 ÷ 7 → 12
1400 ÷ 25 → ×4 both → 5600 ÷ 100 → 56
450 ÷ 15 → ×2 both → 900 ÷ 30 → 30

c) Chunk it. For 1-digit divisors, take big friendly bites left to right.
5208 ÷ 8 → 8×600=4800, leaves 408 → 8×50=400, leaves 8 → 8×1 → 651
Remainder questions stop right there: 5210 ÷ 8 → 651 remainder 2.

Before any of it, size the answer. 5208÷8 → 8×600=4800, 8×700=5600, so it's in the 600s. Being off by a factor of 10 is the single most common division mistake in an OA, and this one habit kills it.
7
Chains: finish one thing, then park it — for (1222 × 56) + 888 the arithmetic isn't the hard part. Holding a number while doing more arithmetic is.

The rule: never interleave. Finish the multiplication completely, say the result to yourself so it's locked, and only then touch the ± part.
1222 × 56 → 1222×50 = 61100, 1222×6 = 7332 → 68432. Park it. → +888 → 69320
A half-finished multiplication plus a running addition is where the error actually comes from — not from the multiplying.

Absorb the tail when it's a multiple. (34 × 56) + 68 → 68 is 2×34 → 34 × 58 → 1972. Only when it's obvious; don't hunt for it.

MCQ killer — check the last digit first. (1222×56)+888 → 2×6 ends in 2; 2+8 ends in 0. The answer ends in 0. Scan the options; most die before you compute anything.

⭐ Divisor splits — the table that fixes division

Move 6a only works if the split is instant. These are the exact divisors the Drill Arena throws at you, so learn this row-by-row and the ➗ mode stops needing hints.

÷12 = ÷4 ÷314 = ÷2 ÷715 = ÷3 ÷516 = halve ×418 = ÷2 ÷9
÷21 = ÷3 ÷724 = ÷8 ÷327 = ÷3 ÷928 = ÷4 ÷732 = ÷4 ÷8
÷35 = ÷5 ÷736 = ÷4 ÷942 = ÷6 ÷745 = ÷9 ÷548 = ÷6 ÷8
÷54 = ÷6 ÷956 = ÷8 ÷763 = ÷9 ÷772 = ÷8 ÷984 = ÷12 ÷7

Always take the easier factor first — 1584÷36 is much nicer as ÷4 then ÷9 (396→44) than ÷9 then ÷4. The exceptions: 11, 13, 17, 19 are prime — no split exists, so chunk them (move 6c). And 25 / 125 aren't worth splitting: use ×4÷100 and ×8÷1000.

⚡ Speed tricks by operation

Multiply by 11

52 × 11 → write 5_2, middle = 5+2 → 572. Carry if needed: 68×11 → 6_(14)_8 → 748.

Multiply by 9 / 99

47 × 9 → 47×10 − 47 → 423. Same idea: ×99 = ×100 − once.

Squares ending in 5 (magic one)

35² → first digit × (itself+1), stick 25 on: 3×4=12 → 1225. 75² → 7×8=56 → 5625. Ten seconds saved, every time.

Squares near a known one

21² = 20² + 20 + 21 = 441. 19² = 20² − 20 − 19 = 361. (n±1)² without the formula stress.

Percent flip

x% of y = y% of x. 4% of 75 feels hard → 75% of 4 = 3. Instant.

Division by 5, 25, 125

÷5 → ×2 then ÷10: 435 ÷ 5 → 870/10 → 87. ÷25 → ×4 ÷100. ÷125 → ×8 ÷1000.

Why it works: 5 = 10/2, so dividing by 5 is dividing by 10 and undoing the halving. Same logic gives you ÷50 → ×2 ÷100.

➗ Division — the weak-point section

Four tricks, in the order you should reach for them. Everything here is move 6 made concrete.

1. Size it before you solve it

4728 ÷ 8 → 8×500=4000, 8×600=4800. So the answer is in the 500s, and it's close to 600. Now compute: 591. If you'd got 59 or 5910 you'd catch it instantly.

Do this every single time. Magnitude errors cost more marks in OAs than arithmetic errors do, because they look like a plausible option.

2. Chunk, don't long-divide

5208 ÷ 8 → strip off 8×600 = 4800, leaving 408 → 8×50 = 400, leaving 8 → 8×1. Answer 600 + 50 + 1 = 651.

Big bite first, then clean up. You're allowed to take clumsy chunks — 8×600 then 8×50 then 8×1 is exactly as correct as elegant long division, and far easier to hold in your head.

3. Split the divisor (2-digit divisors)

1584 ÷ 36 → ÷4 → 396 → ÷9 → 44. 4032 ÷ 56 → ÷8 → 504 → ÷7 → 72.

Needs the divisor-splits table on the Memorize page. Easier factor first — dividing by 4 before 9 keeps the intermediate number friendly.

4. Scale both sides

Multiply or divide both numbers and the answer is untouched. 1400 ÷ 25 → ×4 both → 5600 ÷ 100 → 56. 168 ÷ 14 → halve both → 84 ÷ 7 → 12.

Best move when the divisor is near a friendly number. Aim the scaling at making the divisor 10, 100, or a single digit.

✖️ Multiplier shortcuts (the ones the heavy drills use)

×15 → ×10 plus half of it. 1222 × 15 → 12220 + 6110 → 18330
×25 → ×100 then ÷4. 1222 × 25 → 122200/4 → 30550
×45 → ×90 then halve. 1222 × 45 → 109980/2 → 54990
×99 → ×100 minus once. 1222 × 99 → 122200 − 1222 → 120978
The ×n1 family (21, 31, 41, 51, 61) → ×(the round part), then add the number once.
1222 × 51 → 61100 + 1222 → 62322

Verification #1: the last-digit check (2 seconds)

Multiply only the units digits, then apply the ± tail. (1222 × 56) + 888 → 2×6 → ends in 2 → 2+8 → ends in 0. Any option not ending in 0 is dead without a single real calculation.

This is the highest return-per-second technique on the whole page. Use it before computing, to shrink the options — not after, to check.

Verification #2: digit sums (casting out nines)

Reduce every number to a single digit by summing digits repeatedly, then do the same operations on those digits. If the results don't match, you made an error.

(1222 × 56) + 888 = 69320?
1222→7 · 56→11→2 · 7×2=14→5 · 888→24→6 · 5+6=11→2
Answer 69320 → 6+9+3+2+0 = 20 → 2 ✔ match.

Catches almost every slip except digit-swaps (it won't spot 69320 vs 69302). Worth the 5 seconds on a question you're unsure of — not on every question.

The exam meta-trick: estimate → eliminate

MCQs don't need exact answers. 289 × 31? That's ≈ 290×30 ≈ 8700 — if only one option is near 8700, you're done. Options are your friends; check them before calculating precisely.

📅 The 5-minute daily routine

Mental math is a muscle, not knowledge — 5 minutes daily beats 2 hours once. Until placements end:

  • Min 1–2: Arena ➗ division — double time, because it's the weakest link. Watch whether the 💡 hint appears; that's your real score.
  • Min 3: Arena 🧩 compound — three or four questions. Practise parking the product before doing the ± (move 7).
  • Min 4: Recite from memory — alternate days between the fraction↔% table and the divisor-splits table.
  • Min 5: Alternate days: "% of number" ↔ "profit chains" — the exact skill placement MCQs test.
How to know division is fixed: you're done with the double-allocation when you can clear ten ➗ questions in a row without the hint line appearing and without pausing to picture long division. Then drop division to one minute and give the freed minute back to compound.
Real-life reps (free practice): mess prices (add your tray total before the cashier does), cricket strike rates, splitting bills, discount tags in shops. Every number you see is a rep.
Exam-day rule: if a calculation takes more than 3 steps in your head, you're solving it the wrong way — look for the trick (fraction form, round-and-fix, or estimate-and-eliminate). Placement questions are DESIGNED to have a shortcut.
